3D surface view for grid tables

Any grid-shaped table can now be viewed as a 3D surface next to its heatmap — editable type-7/17 maps and read-only Calculated Tables alike. The point is spotting the spikes and dips in a calibration that are genuinely hard to see as a wall of numbers.

Open a grid table and click 3D View above the grid.

  • Left-drag rotates the surface; right-drag pans it.
  • + / − zoom, and Reset returns to the default view.
  • Click a point on the surface and the matching cell is ringed in the 2D grid beside it — so a spike you can see but not place resolves to an exact RPM/MAP cell.

The surface and the grid sit side by side rather than stacked, so you can read one against the other without either pushing the other off screen.

A surface, not a bar chart

Each cell is a vertex of one continuous shaded mesh rather than its own block, so the slope between cells is visible. That interpolation ramp is usually the thing being judged — a value that looks reasonable on its own can still sit on a step that isn't.

Calculated Tables stay read-only here, exactly as they are everywhere else in the app. Nothing in the 3D view edits a value; it is for reading the calibration, not changing it.

Rendering and responsiveness

The view is rasterized on the CPU, with no GPU or graphics-driver dependency, so it renders identically on every machine rather than depending on which backend a given system happens to provide.

Rotate and pan were reworked late in this cycle. Moving the camera used to rebuild the 2D grid alongside the surface on every frame — a full repaint of all 527 cells on a 31×17 table, to redraw a grid whose contents cannot change when only the camera moves. Camera changes now repaint the surface alone, which took the measured time between frames during a drag down by roughly a factor of six.

Verified on the machine that built it: formatting, lints, the full workspace test suite (24 suites, 330 tests), the interface-glyph check, and a signed release build driven by hand against a real 31×17 calibration table — rotating, panning, zooming, and clicking points on the surface to confirm the right cell rings in the grid. The responsiveness figure above is a measured before/after on that same table, not an estimate.
